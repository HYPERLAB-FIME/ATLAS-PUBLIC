Abstract

ATLAS the protocol moves scoped evidence to an authorized Assessor and returns a signed result handle. This explainer is the place for why that is useful and for how a deploying scheme might interpret or consume a verdict — including intent faithfulness, fairness or steering checks, and regulatory programmes. None of that is standardised by ATLAS itself.

1. What this document is

Treat every example below as illustration. A regulatory-compliance pack, intent-matching verification, a card network, or a national supervisor might consume ATLAS results in very different ways. A programme may bind roster assessment_types and policy_referential to certified methods. ATLAS only requires that the Assessor decrypt in-scope evidence, apply the method its authorization names, and issue Link 4 / Link 5.

Keep this split in mind:

2. Why independent assessment exists

Agentic commerce converts natural-language user intent into checkout artifacts through an autonomous subject agent. Neighbouring protocols (AP2, Verifiable Intent, UCP, KYA-OS) secure identity, mandate, and checkout. They do not, by themselves, give merchants, processors, networks, or issuers a standard way to observe pre-checkout behaviour.

The operational gap is opacity: between the user’s instruction and the payment action the agent’s decisions are probabilistic and can drift without any change to static credentials. ATLAS exists so an independent Assessor can obtain bounded, encrypted evidence and emit a compact signed signal. What that signal is for is a scheme choice.

3. Illustrative drift (intent)

One common story — not a required ATLAS method — is intent drift: the agent’s interpretation of constraints (budget, allergens, ethical preferences, medical limitations) diverges from what the user actually asked, across interpretation, tool serialisation, and safety-filter layers.

Interpretation.
An LLM maps free text to an internal representation. Rare or medically nuanced constraints may be under-weighted.
Structuring.
Tool calls (MCP or similar) force a schema. Fields that do not map cleanly can be dropped.
Filtering and presentation.
PII or safety filters can redact the very constraints that mattered; the confirmation screen still “looks right.”

A user says: “Order a pizza. I am severely allergic to peanuts, I am on a particular heart medication, and my budget is 40 €.” The agent may encode "max_price": 40 cleanly while the allergy and drug-interaction constraint survive only as vague text — or are stripped — before add_to_cart. The screen shows “vegetarian pizza, 40 €”. If harm follows, no party holds an assessable record of what the agent understood unless evidence was released under ATLAS scope and a scheme method actually checks those constraints.

ATLAS can carry dialogue, traces, or structured slots so such a method can run. It does not require a health-constraint check, and it does not define “intent accuracy.”

4. How a scheme may consume a result

After Link 4 exists, relying parties decide what to do with verdict, digests, and optional report_token. The protocol stops at verification of signatures and assembly of redeem_tokens. The following uses are examples of programme policy, not ATLAS requirements:

  • Authorisation policy. A network or issuer may treat not_compliant or not_assessed differently from compliant when deciding to approve, step-up, or decline.
  • Chargeback and dispute files. A signed, time-stamped result can be stored as a common reference. Whether it shifts liability, or is even admissible, is rulebook and law — not ATLAS.
  • Fraud operations. Verdicts over time for one agent.id may feed monitoring. Thresholds, suspension, and re-attestation are operator policy.
  • Offer terms (UCP-style). A merchant might change price or acceptance during negotiation when a result is present. ATLAS does not require that.
  • Regulatory demonstration. A programme might map a named policy_referential to obligations under instruments such as the EU AI Act, PSD2/PSD3, DORA, or the Interchange Fee Regulation. ATLAS does not implement those laws and does not certify compliance with them.

Empty scope yields protocol not_assessed so rails can see that no agent-behaviour method ran. Whether a scheme then declines the transaction is again consumption policy.

5. Example method packs

Roster assessment_types are opaque codes. A scheme profile might publish packs such as:

  • Intent-matching verification — compare released dialogue / slots / traces to the cart or mandate.
  • Fairness / steering — look at consideration sets, affiliate identifiers, or ranking traces if those classes were released.
  • Regulatory compliance — apply a certified checklist the scheme names; ATLAS does not author the checklist.

An Assessor may record an optional evidence_cross_check on Link 4 (cart lines vs item_selection_reasoning, mandate limits vs checkout). Whether a material_mismatch becomes not_compliant is defined by the active referential, not by ATLAS. Preferring a holder_commissioned copy of the same artifact_id over an agent_observed copy is likewise a method choice (often used when a later dispute file should cite the holder’s attestation).

6. KYA complementarity

Know Your Agent frameworks typically bind identity and keys before runtime. The same agent often has a different id and status in each network’s KYA store. The protocol field registered_identities[] is how the Assessor is told which stores to query for the requester; whether a scheme then shares only a minimal “lower trust” signal is programme policy. ATLAS evidence is otherwise about what happened in a session. A scheme may feed AssessmentResult hashes into KYA monitoring so drift that never appears in static credentials becomes visible. That closed loop — scores, re-attestation, suspension — is operations on top of ATLAS, not a protocol step.
